Frequently asked
Is my photo actually private, or does it get uploaded somewhere?
Everything happens inside this browser tab. There's no server this tool talks to — the page even overrides fetch and XMLHttpRequest on load, so any network call, intentional or not, gets silently blocked rather than sent. Check your browser's own network tab if you want to see for yourself.
My photo shows no GPS even though location was on. Is that a bug?
Usually not. Some cameras write a GPS tag with placeholder (0,0) coordinates when location tagging is enabled but no satellite fix was acquired yet — common right when opening the camera, or indoors. You'll see that flagged as "no location fix" rather than a real coordinate. Genuinely missing GPS just means the camera never wrote a location tag at all.
What does "Verified clean" actually mean?
After cleaning a file, this tool re-scans the result with the exact same detector that found the original metadata. "0 fields remain" is a measured result from that re-scan, not an assumption — if anything survives, it shows up as "partial" instead, telling you honestly rather than claiming success either way.
Why can't I convert to GIF, BMP, SVG, or ICO?
Those can all be read and cleaned as a source file, but no browser can encode to them from a canvas — that capability just doesn't exist in the platform. GIF is the exception: it's supported as a conversion target because this page includes its own from-scratch GIF encoder, built specifically since browsers won't do it natively.
What's the difference between the three modes?
Inspect is read-only — see what's embedded without changing anything. Remove metadata strips it while keeping your original format, byte-for-byte lossless. Convert format changes the file type (which also strips metadata as a side effect, but recompresses the image in the process).
Is this really free, with no signup or limits?
Yes. There's no account, no email, no server-side processing to meter or pay for — the heavy lifting happens on your own device, so there's nothing that scales cost with usage. Use it on as many files as you want.
Why keep the color profile if it's "metadata"?
A color profile (ICC) affects how colors render on screen — it's not a record of who took the photo or where. Removing it can shift how an image looks (especially from cameras shooting in a wide color gamut), so it's deliberately left alone while the actually identifying tags — GPS, camera serials, author info — are removed.
Does HEIC/AVIF work the same as JPEG or PNG?
Reading and cleaning HEIC/AVIF works the same way (metadata is zeroed out in place rather than cut, which is safer against corrupting those formats). Converting to or from HEIC depends on your browser's own decoder — mainly Safari supports it natively. If your browser can't handle it, that file falls back to a lossless clean instead of failing silently.
How do I see exactly what's in a file, or hide part of a photo?
Tap any row with fields found to expand it — a GPS tag shows real decoded coordinates with a map link, not just "present." For hiding something visible in the photo itself (a face, a license plate, a document in the background), the small redact button on eligible rows opens an editor where you draw black boxes directly onto the image. That's burned into the pixels when applied, which is a different, stronger kind of privacy than metadata removal — there's nothing left to find or strip afterward.
